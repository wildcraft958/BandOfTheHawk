# Externally-Sourced Parameters for a Payment-Fraud Red/Blue Adversarial Simulation

## TL;DR
- **Priority 1 (GenAI-attacker anchoring) is well-served by benchmark data**: ASVspoof (2015→2024/ASVspoof5) and deepfake/AI-text detection leaderboards give clean, ordered, externally-anchored operating points (EER, min-tDCF, AUC/AUROC, APCER/BPCER) to map capability tiers to real tool generations. The single most important unanswered question — the benign joint probability of password-reset + auth-call + new-device-binding within one hour — is GENUINELY UNAVAILABLE and must be swept.
- **Priorities 2–5 split sharply**: card-fraud base rates, CNP shares, and fraud-type mixes are excellent from regulators (Federal Reserve, ECB/EBA, UK Finance); but operating-point micro-parameters (3DS challenge/abandonment, false-decline rate, manual-review cost, AVS/CVV conditional distributions) are dominated by vendor/industry estimates and must be labeled and swept, not cited as measured.
- **The multi-detector correlation question has a partial but decisive answer**: published deepfake-ensemble papers show detector scores are positively but sub-unity correlated on the *same* samples (pairwise ρ ≈ 0.90–0.92), so the user's instinct that ~17 independent per-tier draws is unrealistic is correct — draws should be positively correlated within a tool-quality tier, with a Gaussian copula and swept ρ.

## Key Findings
The research strongly supports (a) replacing independent per-detector sampling with a positively-correlated multivariate draw, and (b) using ASVspoof cross-year EERs / deepfake AUC-by-year / RAID AUROC-by-generator as ordinal tier anchors. Regulator data anchors fraud base rates precisely (US Federal Reserve, ECB/EBA, UK Finance). Operating-point micro-parameters are the weakest tier — almost entirely vendor/industry-sourced — and belong in a sensitivity sweep. The most decision-critical parameter in the whole task (the benign ATO event-chain joint probability) does not exist in any published form and must be swept over 10⁻⁷–10⁻⁴ per user-day.

## PRIORITY 1 — Capability-tier anchoring for the GenAI attacker

### 1.1 Voice cloning vs. ASV / speech-deepfake detection (ASVspoof across years)
Ordered anchors (EER = equal error rate; higher EER = attacker tool defeats detector more often). All **MEASURED** (benchmark-challenge results):
- **ASVspoof 2015 (LA, TTS/VC)**: best deep-feature systems reached ~0.0% EER on seen attacks S1–S9; ~1.1% averaged including the harder unseen S10. Population: clean logical-access synthetic speech, 2015-era TTS/VC. Source: ASVspoof 2015 summary (Wu et al., Interspeech 2015; asvspoof.org/is2015_asvspoof.pdf).
- **ASVspoof 2017 (PA, replay)**: baseline replay countermeasure EER 24.65% (v2.0: 24.77%); baseline ASV EER rose from 1.76% to 30.71% (v2.0: 31.46%) under replay. 49 teams. Source: Kinnunen et al., "The ASVspoof 2017 Challenge," Interspeech 2017.
- **ASVspoof 2019 (LA)**: best single system min-tDCF 0.2093, EER 11.40%. Source: Todisco et al., "ASVspoof 2019: Future Horizons," Interspeech 2019; summary arXiv 2102.05889.
- **ASVspoof 2021 (LA & DF)**: 54 teams. LA top evaluation-set pooled EER in the low-single-digit region for the best systems; baseline B03 pooled EER 44.77% eval. DF (deepfake) track top eval EER 15.64% (T23); most systems clustered 16–25%. Source: Liu et al., "ASVspoof 2021: Towards Spoofed and Deepfake Speech Detection in the Wild," arXiv 2210.02437, Tables XIV & XVI.
- **ASVspoof 5 / 2024 (crowdsourced MLS speech, latest-gen TTS/VC + adversarial attacks)**: top open-condition single-system Track 1 EER 4.16% (minDCF 0.1124; Whispeak, ISCA ASVspoof 2024, Falez & Marteau); a sparse-detection AASIST baseline reported 23.36% EER on the ASVspoof5 test set. Source: Wang et al., "ASVspoof 5," arXiv 2408.08739; ScienceDirect S0885230825000506.

**Tier-mapping guidance (ordinal, MEASURED anchors):** Tier-1 (weak attacker) → detector EER ~1–5% (2015/2019-era attacks vs. matched detectors); Tier-2 (mid) → EER ~10–16% (2021 DF track, cross-condition); Tier-3 (strong / generalization-gap) → EER ~20–24% (ASVspoof5 hard / out-of-domain, 2024-era zero-shot TTS). These are detector-EER-against-tool, precisely the ordered external anchors requested. **Caveat:** organizers explicitly warn cross-edition EERs are not cardinally comparable (different databases, protocols, and metric optimization); use them as ordinal tier anchors only.

### 1.2 Face/liveness deepfakes vs. PAD / deepfake detection
- **NIST FATE Part 10 (PAD), NIST IR 8491 (2023)**: evaluated 82 passive software-based 2D face PAD algorithms across 9 attack types (16+ result tables). Metrics per ISO/IEC 30107-3: APCER@BPCER=0.01 ("convenience") and BPCER@APCER=0.01 ("security"). No single leaderboard metric — results are reported PA-type-by-PA-type. **MEASURED** (US government benchmark). Source: NIST IR 8491, nist.gov.
- **Deepfake-detection AUC by generation (intra-dataset, MEASURED, peer-reviewed)**: cross-generational table (arXiv 2207.06612, Table 1) gives ordered anchors by method/year — Two-stream (2017) FF++ 70.7 / DFDC 61.4 / Celeb-DF 53.8; MesoNet (2018) 84.7/75.3/54.8; Warp-Art (2019) 93.0/75.5/64.6; modern transformer ~99.8/99.1/97.2. Cross-dataset generalization collapses (FF++→Celeb-DF often 60–80% AUC), which is the more realistic attacker-vs-detector regime. Celeb-DF (Li et al., CVPR 2020) is the hardest/most realistic 2nd-gen generation; first-gen detectors score only ~53–65% AUC on it.

**Tier mapping:** Tier-1 → detector AUC ~95–99% (old GAN/faceswap vs. modern detector, intra-dataset); Tier-2 → AUC ~80–90% (cross-dataset, 2nd-gen); Tier-3 → AUC ~60–75% (unseen 2nd/3rd-gen, cross-domain). For FAR/FRR operating points, anchor to NIST FATE convenience/security points (APCER@BPCER=0.01, BPCER@APCER=0.01).

### 1.3 Document forensics (fake ID / doc fraud) vs. detectors
Thinly served by public benchmarks. There is **no NIST-FATE-equivalent public leaderboard** with clean accuracy-by-generation-year for synthetic/forged identity documents comparable to ASVspoof or FATE PAD. This is a **GENUINELY UNAVAILABLE** tier (see final lists) — sweep detector AUC across tiers.

### 1.4 LLM-generated text vs. AI-text detectors
- **RAID benchmark (Dugan et al., ACL 2024; arXiv 2405.07940)**: >6M generations, 11 models, 8 domains, 11 adversarial attacks, 4 decoding strategies. **MEASURED.** Detector AUROC on RAID (reproduced in DetectZoo, arXiv 2606.04205, Table 11): RADAR 0.826, Fast-DetectGPT 0.787, Glimpse 0.764, LRR 0.750, Binoculars 0.739, GhostBuster 0.653, DNA-GPT 0.653, several below 0.5. Key published finding: commercial/open detectors claiming ≥99% accuracy fall sharply under sampling variation and adversarial attacks.
- Generation-by-model ordering exists in RAID (gpt2 → gpt3 → chatgpt/gpt4) enabling tier mapping.

**Tier mapping:** Tier-1 (GPT-2-era, default decoding) → AUROC ~0.95+; Tier-2 (GPT-3.5 / instruction-tuned) → ~0.8; Tier-3 (frontier + adversarial/paraphrase decoding) → ~0.5–0.75.

### 1.5 CRITICAL: do multiple detector scores on the SAME artifact correlate?
**Answer: Yes — positively but sub-unity correlated, per multiple peer-reviewed deepfake-ensemble studies. The user's suspicion is correct; ~17 independent draws is unrealistic.**
- Pairwise prediction correlations among complementary deepfake streams reported at ρ ≈ 0.906–0.921 (Global-Crop 0.916, Global-Fusion 0.921, Crop-Fusion 0.906) — strongly positive on the same samples, but with meaningful residual independence enabling ensemble gains. Source: "Robust Deepfake Detection: Mitigating Spatial Attention Drift via Calibrated Complementary Ensembles," arXiv 2604.25889, Fig. 4.
- Ensembling complementary detectors raises AUROC (e.g., BlenD 91.3% + FS-VFM 90% → 94%), confirming errors are correlated but not identical. Source: "The Alpha Blending Hypothesis," arXiv 2605.10334, Tables 5–6.
- Cross-dataset, uncertainty/error correlations collapse (in-domain ρ ≈ 0.44 → external ρ ≈ 0.04), showing correlation structure is domain-dependent, not stable. Source: "Architecture-Adaptive Uncertainty Fusion for Deepfake Detection," arXiv 2606.06666.

**Design recommendation:** Replace independent per-tier draws with a positively-correlated multivariate draw (Gaussian copula, within-modality pairwise ρ ≈ 0.6–0.9 within a tool-quality tier; cross-modality ρ ≈ 0.1–0.3). Sweep ρ. This is the single most defensible change to the capability model.

## PRIORITY 2 — Fraud-detection operating points

- **CNP false-decline rate**: the average online store declined 2.6% of all incoming orders due to fraud concerns, including declining 3.1% of all orders valued over $100. Source: **Merchant Risk Council 2017 Global Fraud Survey** (via SEON/ClearSale). Population: e-commerce/CNP. Confidence: **INDUSTRY ESTIMATE** (survey). A commonly repeated claim that ~10% of e-commerce dollars are declined with up to 70% being legitimate is an aggregator estimate — flag and sweep.
- **Healthy overall CNP decline rate**: 5–10% "healthy," >15% signals a problem. Source: payments vendor (Slash). Confidence: **VENDOR/INDUSTRY ESTIMATE.**
- **3DS2 frictionless vs. challenge vs. abandonment**: well-tuned US e-commerce ≈ 75–85% frictionless, 10–15% challenge-with-completion, 3–5% challenge-step abandonment, 1–2% technical failure (KeyTransact). Corroborating: ~80–85% frictionless when full data sent (pci-proxy); challenge-step abandonment 10–30% depending on implementation (gr4vy). Confidence: **VENDOR/INDUSTRY ESTIMATE** — sweep.
- **Step-up / challenge friction cost**: challenge flows reduce conversion ~10–18% on average (Beast Insights, citing Stripe benchmarks). Confidence: **VENDOR/INDUSTRY ESTIMATE** — sweep.
- **AVS/CVV match distributions conditional on legit vs. fraud**: no clean regulator/network public distribution found. **GENUINELY UNAVAILABLE** — sweep.
- **Payee cooling-off**: UK Confirmation-of-Payee and APP-reimbursement regime context: **PSR mandatory APP reimbursement live 7 Oct 2024**, applies to payments up to **£85,000** via Faster Payments and CHAPS, with **liability split 50/50 between sending and receiving banks**; PSR preliminary data show **86% of in-scope APP-scam losses returned to victims Oct–Dec 2024.** Source: PSR / UK Finance (regulator). Confidence: policy existence = **MEASURED**; specific new-payee holding-period *hours* were not pinned to a single primary bank-policy document — treat specific hold durations as **UNAVAILABLE / sweep.**
- **Manual fraud-review cost per case**: no regulator/network figure exists; commonly cited industry benchmarks are vendor-sourced with undisclosed methodology. **GENUINELY UNAVAILABLE** from a credible primary source — sweep.
- **ASV EER-based operating thresholds**: use ASVspoof min-tDCF/EER operating points (§1.1) as published DET-curve anchors. **MEASURED.**

## PRIORITY 3 — Consumer payment behavior distributions

- **Transaction-amount tail**: best characterization is a **log-normal body + Pareto/power-law tail (mixture)**. Peer-reviewed support: consumer expenditure follows a mixture of log-normal and Pareto with the Pareto tail comprising ~10–20% of the population (Ghosh, Gangopadhyay & Basu, arXiv 0912.5420; India NSSO 1983–2007; **MEASURED**). AML-simulation practice calibrates log-normal (μ = ln median, σ ≥ 0.6) with a Pareto tail (Tide generator, arXiv 2603.01863, calibrated to the 2022 Federal Reserve Payments Study). Practical anchor: >90% of card purchases below $100, a small fraction >$1,000 (arXiv 2506.10842 EDA). **Recommendation:** model amounts as log-normal core + Pareto tail; sweep tail index α ∈ [1.05, 3].
- **Average transaction values (MEASURED, Federal Reserve / Nilson)**: the average credit card transaction in 2024 was **$83**, the average debit card transaction **$70** (Federal Reserve). Credit cards = **35%** of consumer transactions, debit = **30%** (Federal Reserve Diary of Consumer Payment Choice, October 2024; via Motley Fool / Nilson Report).
- **Transactions per cardholder**: the average US credit cardholder makes **up to 264 credit card purchases per year** (Capital One Shopping, Credit Card Ownership Statistics 2026, compiled from Federal Reserve Bank of New York Center for Microeconomic Data; a 2026 update revises this to ~271/yr). Confidence: **INDUSTRY ESTIMATE.** Over-dispersion / negative-binomial parameters are not published — sweep (NB with mean ≈ 264/yr, sweep dispersion).
- **Inter-arrival / burstiness (Hawkes)**: no published Hawkes-process characterization of consumer card sequences located. **GENUINELY UNAVAILABLE** — sweep branching ratio.
- **MCC / merchant-popularity distribution**: heavy-tailed/Zipfian is standard practice but no single card-network study was pinned; consumer-electronics sales fit lognormal-with-power-law-tail (arXiv 1004.0637) as a **PROXY**.
- **Cards held per cardholder (US)** — reconcile by definition: Experian ~3.9 cards (2024); Nilson ~4.9 cards among accountholders; Experian 2025 ~7.1 held / ~3.7 active. Distribution (Capital One Shopping, from Fed NY / public data): ~20% zero cards, 31% one, 29% two, 12% three; by generation Gen Z 2.2, Silent 3.1. **The commonly cited "~2.3" average is outdated** — the current US average is materially higher and depends on the held-vs-active definition. 74–81% of US adults hold ≥1 credit card (Federal Reserve). Confidence: **MEASURED / INDUSTRY ESTIMATE** mix.
- **Credit line by income / account tenure / device replacement cycle**: partial — average total credit limit ~$33,980 per consumer (Capital One Shopping, **INDUSTRY ESTIMATE**). Income-conditional credit line, account-tenure distributions, and device upgrade cycle were not pinned to primary sources — sweep.
- **CNP vs CP share (MEASURED, regulator)**: Federal Reserve reports CNP transaction *volume* grew 10.7%/yr 2022–23 (down from 18.1%/yr 2009–2022). Use the Federal Reserve Payments Study for transaction-share splits. (Note: the ECB "CNP = ~84% of card fraud" figure is a *fraud-value* share, not a transaction share — do not conflate.)

## PRIORITY 4 — Legitimate/benign payment-graph topology
- **Benign device-to-card fan-out, device sharing, shared-payee among unrelated accounts, and benign payment-graph degree/clustering/density**: no dedicated peer-reviewed characterization of *legitimate* consumer payment-graph topology with a documented "normal vs. suspicious knee" was located. The closest real anchor is IEEE-CIS within-entity structure: 13,553 unique card entities (card1), median 4 and max 14,932 transactions per entity (arXiv 2604.13125). Largely **GENUINELY UNAVAILABLE** — sweep the fan-out knee.

## PRIORITY 5 — Hard-negative base rates & fraud-type mix

- **Overall US card-fraud base rate (MEASURED, Federal Reserve)**: debit-card fraud losses rose from 17.5 bps (2021) to 17.6 bps (2023) of transaction value for dual-message networks; single-message networks 4.2 bps (2021). Source: Federal Reserve Regulation II Interchange Fee reports (2021, 2023), federalreserve.gov.
- **US CP vs CNP fraud rates (MEASURED, Kansas City Fed)**: for dual-message networks in 2023, counterfeit fraud 8.0 bps vs. lost/stolen 4.8 bps. France CNP fraud rose to 3.2% of CNP transactions by 2015 (Bank of France). One 2023-data estimate puts US card-present fraud ~9.9 bps vs. EEA 0.7 bps (aggregator — flag).
- **EU/EEA fraud (MEASURED, ECB/EBA Joint Report on Payment Fraud, EBA/REP/2025/40, 15 Dec 2025)**: total EU/EEA payment fraud **€4.2B (2024)**, up from €3.5B (2023) and €3.4B (2022). Fraudulent **credit transfers €2.5B (fraud rate 0.001%)**; fraudulent **card transactions €1.3B (fraud rate 0.033%)**; card fraud on EU/EEA-issued cards **€1.329B, +29% YoY.** Card fraud was **17× higher when the payment recipient was outside the EEA** (where SCA is not legally required). Earlier: card fraud H1 2023 = 0.031% of value / 0.015% of volume; CNP = ~84% of card-fraud value (2020–21); €1.28B CNP fraud (2021). Source: ECB/EBA joint reports; ECB card-fraud reports.
- **UK fraud-type mix (MEASURED, UK Finance Annual Fraud Report 2025)**: 2024 unauthorised card fraud on UK-issued cards **£572.6M (+4% from £551.3M in 2023)**; APP fraud **£450.7M (−2%; £365.7M personal + £84.9M non-personal)**, of which ~£267.1M (~59% of APP losses) was reimbursed. Remote purchase fraud drove the card rise, cases **+22% to 2.58M incidents**. 3.13M unauthorised-fraud cases in 2024. Customers fully refunded in **>98% of unauthorised fraud cases.** APP mix: purchase scams = 71% of cases but only 19% of losses; investment scams fewer cases but £144.4M losses; 66–72% of APP scams originate online.
- **Friendly-fraud share of chargebacks / chargeback rate by MCC**: not pinned to a card-network primary source. **INDUSTRY ESTIMATE / UNAVAILABLE** — sweep.
- **Password-reset rate / travel frequency / gift-card prevalence**: password-reset self-reported survey data (Statista 2017: ~14% of users changed a banking password within a week, ~35–40% within 3 months) — **INDUSTRY ESTIMATE.** Travel frequency and gift-card prevalence not pinned to primary sources — sweep.
- **MOST IMPORTANT SUB-QUESTION — benign joint P(password-reset ∧ auth/support-call ∧ new-device-binding within ~1 hour)**: **GENUINELY UNAVAILABLE.** After a dedicated targeted search (academic ATO-detection literature; vendors Sift, LexisNexis, Arkose, Incognia; regulators), **no published joint/chained benign base rate exists** — from a bank, card network, academic paper, or regulator. The chain is universally described as a *fraud signal* (e.g., "failed login from new device → password reset → transfer to unfamiliar recipient within 48h, no single event anomalous in isolation," arXiv 2606.25007 — itself a *synthetic* dataset and thus an example of the artifact risk you are trying to rule out) but its benign co-occurrence rate is never measured.
  - ~~**Best MEASURED component (new-device/new-location logins are rare among legit users)**~~ **⚠️ CORRECTED — see `literature_addendum.md` §3.** The claim "new-device login ≈ 1–10% of legit logins" was attributed to Wiefling et al., ACM TOPS 26(1) Art. 6 (2022). **That statistic does not appear in the paper.** Wiefling reports *re-authentication trigger rates* under a risk-scoring model at chosen attacker-blocking thresholds — a model-and-threshold-dependent quantity, not a behavioral base rate. This component is **GENUINELY UNAVAILABLE**, not measured; it belongs in List 3. What the paper does give: login sparsity (mean 3.8 logins/user, median 2, 48.3% log in less than monthly), a "stable setup" reached after only **4–8 historical logins**, and a calibrated likelihood-ratio risk-score architecture (IP 0.6 / ASN 0.3 / country 0.1). Freeman et al., NDSS 2016 (recall 89% at 10% FPR) and Incognia's vendor figure remain as weak upper bounds only.
  - **Password-reset component (INDUSTRY ESTIMATE only)**: Statista 2017 (banking passwords: ~14% within a week; ~35–40% within 3 months); Entrust (~15% weekly / >51% monthly). Per-day ≈ 0.1–2%.
  - **Support/auth-call component (softest; INDUSTRY ESTIMATE)**: order of magnitude ~1 call per account per 1–2 years for a typical retail customer → ~0.1–0.3% per day.
  - **Naïve independent-events same-day product ≈ 2×10⁻⁷ (≈1 in 5M user-days); one-hour window ~1–2 orders of magnitude smaller.** CRITICAL CAVEAT: these three events are positively correlated for legitimate users (the "got a new phone → reset password to log in → called support because locked out" scenario), so the true benign joint rate is **higher** than the independent product — plausibly 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁶ per user-day but **unmeasured**. This is the central caveat for validating the ATO detector.

## PRIORITY 6 — Public datasets & prior simulation frameworks

- **IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection (Vesta, Kaggle 2019)**: 590,540 CNP e-commerce transactions; 394 features (transaction + identity tables merged); 3.5% fraud rate; 13,553 unique card entities (card1). Fields largely masked (Vesta-engineered V1–V339; id_01–id_38 device/network; TransactionDT time delta). Winning private-LB AUC 0.9459. **MEASURED** (real Vesta transactions). Limitation: anonymized features prevent realism inspection; synthetic-generator critiques show these behavioral/velocity signals are hard to reproduce (arXiv 2604.13125). License: Kaggle competition terms (non-commercial/competition use).
- **PaySim (Lopez-Rojas et al., EMSS 2016)**: agent-based mobile-money simulator; Kaggle dataset ~6.36M transactions over 744 hourly steps (30-day sim), 0.13% fraud (8,213 cases), scaled to 1/4 of the source. Fields: step, type (CASH_IN/CASH_OUT/DEBIT/PAYMENT/TRANSFER), amount, nameOrig/nameDest, oldbalanceOrg/newbalanceOrig/oldbalanceDest/newbalanceDest, isFraud, isFlaggedFraud. Based on one month of logs from an African mobile-money provider. Documented limitations: balance columns misleading for flagged/cancelled frauds; fraud only in TRANSFER and CASH_OUT types. License: CC BY-SA (Kaggle ealaxi/paysim1).
- **Sparkov ("Credit Card Transactions Fraud Detection Dataset," Brandon Harris; Kaggle kartik2112)**: fully synthetic (Faker-based generator). Common Kaggle build: 1,000 customers, 800 merchants, Jan 2019–Dec 2020, ~1.85M transactions, 23 features (trans_date_trans_time, cc_num, merchant, category, amt, first/last, gender, street/city/state/zip/lat/long, city_pop, job, dob, trans_num, merch_lat/merch_long, is_fraud). Labeled fraud type: CNP. License: **CC0 / public domain.** Documented limitation: ensemble models score near-perfectly on Sparkov but poorly on real EU data — synthetic patterns are "too learnable" (ResearchGate 379191633); synthetic generators fail to preserve behavioral/velocity/multi-account fraud signals (arXiv 2604.13125).
- **CFPB Consumer Complaint Database**: **CONFIRMED to contain a free-text consumer narrative field** (opt-in, PII-scrubbed per CFPB Scrubbing Standard). >13.8M complaints total; narratives published only with consumer consent after scrubbing. Fields: date received / sent to company, product/sub-product, issue/sub-issue, company, state/ZIP (privacy rules), submission channel, company public response + response category + timeliness, tags (servicemember, older American), and the consumer narrative. **Public domain (US Government).** API returns JSON/CSV/XLS/XLSX; refreshed nightly. Excellent as a real dispute-narrative corpus for text-detector negatives and template-similarity reference. Source: consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints.
- **Benign payment-graph datasets beyond PaySim**: no strong public *real* benign device–card–payee graph dataset located; AML-oriented synthetic generators (Tide, arXiv 2603.01863; TransXion, arXiv 2604.17420) are the closest but are synthetic and money-laundering-focused.
- **Existing simulation frameworks to evaluate as a foundation**: PaySim (agent-based, EMSS 2016) and its lineage; Sparkov (Faker-based generator); AML generators Tide and TransXion. **Recommendation:** evaluate PaySim's agent-based architecture as a structural foundation, but treat both PaySim's and Sparkov's benign behavior as too simplistic; read the arXiv 2604.13125 critique before building, since it documents exactly how synthetic generators fail to preserve the temporal/velocity/multi-account signals your detector depends on.

## Recommendations (staged, with thresholds)
1. **Immediately adopt correlated detector-score sampling.** Replace ~17 independent per-tier draws with a Gaussian-copula draw at within-modality pairwise ρ ≈ 0.6–0.9 (anchored to the observed ~0.90–0.92 deepfake-stream correlations) and cross-modality ρ ≈ 0.1–0.3. **Sweep ρ.** *Threshold that changes the recommendation:* if any headline result flips between ρ = 0.3 and ρ = 0.9, correlation is a load-bearing assumption and must be empirically measured (e.g., by running several open detectors on the same synthetic artifacts) rather than assumed.
2. **Anchor capability tiers to the benchmark EER/AUC/AUROC ladders** in §1.1–1.4 (ASVspoof 2015→2024; deepfake AUC-by-year; RAID AUROC-by-generator). Use the numbers directly as ordinal tiers.
3. **Treat operating-point micro-parameters (3DS challenge/abandonment, false-decline, review cost, AVS/CVV) as swept nuisance parameters**, never as citable measured values — they are vendor/industry-sourced.
4. **For the ATO joint probability, run a sensitivity sweep over 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁴ per user-day** (lower bound = independent-events product; upper bound = positive-correlation "new-phone-lockout" scenario). Only claim the detector "measures real signal" if fraud-vs-benign separation holds across the *entire* range.
5. **Use CFPB narratives as your real text corpus; use IEEE-CIS as your real transaction anchor; do not trust Sparkov/PaySim benign behavior** as ground truth for legitimate patterns.

## Caveats
- Many Priority-2 operating points come from payment-vendor blogs; they are labeled INDUSTRY ESTIMATE / VENDOR CLAIM and belong in the sweep, not in citable claims.
- ASVspoof cross-year EERs are not cardinally comparable (different databases, protocols, metric optimization) — challenge organizers explicitly warn against direct cross-edition comparison. Use as ordinal tiers only.
- Cards-per-cardholder figures conflict across sources (3.9 vs 4.9 vs 7.1) depending on definition (held vs. active; per-person vs. per-accountholder) — reconcile by definition before use.
- The ECB "CNP ≈ 84% of card fraud" figure is a fraud-*value* share, not a transaction share; the Federal Reserve Payments Study is the correct source for transaction-mode shares.
